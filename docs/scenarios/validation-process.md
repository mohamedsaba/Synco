# Scenario validation process

A scenario advances only through evidence that it is comprehensible, technically sound, fair, and capable of producing meaningful variation.

1. **Paper design:** Complete the template and review realism, hypotheses, forks, shallow approaches, verification, and scope.
2. **Comprehension test:** Ask representative engineers to restate the symptom, expected behavior, and task after reading the brief. Target 60–90 seconds without revealing the root cause.
3. **Technical validation:** Reproduce the initial state, confirm evidence accuracy, validate setup, and test intended and alternative fixes.
4. **Shallow approach simulation:** Follow the most plausible incomplete fix and record what it changes, what passes, and what remains wrong.
5. **Competent approach simulation:** Follow a sound investigation and correction without assuming the author's preferred sequence.
6. **Deep approach simulation:** Explore edge cases and consequence radius to ensure deeper work is possible but not artificially required.
7. **Reconstruction comparison:** Confirm the event histories are chronologically legible, evidence-grounded, and meaningfully distinguishable without evaluative labels.
8. **Fairness review:** Check wording, setup, stack familiarity, hidden assumptions, time budget, accessibility, and accidental traps.
9. **Real candidate pilot:** Observe representative candidates, collect qualitative feedback, and revise or reject the scenario before operational use.

The primary success question is: **Does the scenario produce distinguishable work histories?** Passing tests alone does not validate a scenario, and validation does not define a candidate verdict.
